How to do a donkey kick

Keep your knee bent and press the sole of your foot up.

With coach Sarah

Works
Glutes
Beginners start with
10 reps
Equipment
None

Step by step

  1. On all fours Hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
  2. Kick one foot up Knee stays bent, sole of the foot to the ceiling.
  3. Squeeze your glute Without arching your lower back.
  4. Lower with control Then switch legs.

Common mistakes

  • Arching your lower back to lift higher. Lift only as high as you can while your back stays still. The squeeze in your glute matters more than the height of your foot.
  • Swinging. Kicking with momentum hands the work to your back. Pause for a moment at the top and lower with control.
  • Straightening your knee. Keep your knee bent and press the sole of your foot toward the ceiling, as if you were stamping a footprint on it.
  • Hips twisting open. Keep both hips facing the floor as your leg lifts.
  • Sinking into your shoulders. Push the floor away with your hands so your upper back stays strong.

Muscles it works and why it matters

The donkey kick works your glutes, mainly the large muscle that drives your hip back, the same job it does when you climb stairs or stand up. Working one side at a time lets you feel each glute on its own, which helps if one side tends to do less. It's a staple of at-home glute training because it needs nothing but the floor.

How many and how often

Start with 10 reps on each side, two or three sets, two or three days a week with a rest day in between. Pause for a second or two at the top of each rep. When 10 a side feel easy, slow the lowering down. The next rung up is the fire hydrant, which works the side of your hip; if kicks are too much today, go back to the glute bridge.

Who it's good for, and when to go easier

It's a good next step once bridges feel easy, and it's quiet, so it suits apartments and nap times. You're on your hands and knees, so a folded towel under your knees helps. If your wrists or knees need a break, the standing kickback works the same muscles without either on the floor.

If your lower back aches, make the kick smaller; pain that sticks around is worth showing a doctor or physical therapist. If you're pregnant or recently had a baby, check with your doctor or midwife before you start.
