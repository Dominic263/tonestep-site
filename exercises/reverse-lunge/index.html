How to do a reverse lunge

Step back softly. Front knee stays over your ankle.

With coach Jess

Works
Glutes, thighs
Beginners start with
8 reps
Equipment
None

Step by step

  1. Stand tall Feet hip-width apart, hands on your hips.
  2. Step back softly Lower until both knees bend to about ninety degrees.
  3. Front knee over your ankle Chest up, back knee hovering just above the floor.
  4. Push through your front heel Step back to standing, then switch legs.

Common mistakes

  • Stepping back too short. Your front knee drifts forward and your heel lifts. Step back far enough that both knees bend to about ninety degrees, with your front knee over your ankle.
  • Dropping the back knee. Step back softly and stop with your back knee hovering just above the floor, not landing on it.
  • Feet on a tightrope. Stepping straight behind your front foot makes you wobble. Keep your feet hip-width apart, like standing on two train tracks.
  • Leaning forward. Keep your chest up and your hands on your hips, so you drop straight down rather than tipping over your front leg.
  • Pushing off the back foot. Drive through your front heel to stand. That's where the glute and thigh work comes from.

Muscles it works and why it matters

The reverse lunge works your glutes and thighs one leg at a time, and asks a little of your balance as well. Working each side on its own means your stronger leg can't quietly take over, the way it can in a squat. Stepping back rather than forward is usually easier to control, which is why it comes before other lunges.

How many and how often

Start with 8 reps on each side, two or three sets, two or three days a week with a rest day in between. You can do all your reps on one leg and then switch, or alternate. Holding a wall or a chair for balance is fine while you learn it. When 8 a side feel steady, take a slower way down; the next rung up is the split squat. If lunges are too much today, go back to the squat.

Who it's good for, and when to go easier

It's the next step for anyone whose squats feel easy, and a good move for stairs and walking uphill. Lunges ask a lot of your knees, so make the lunge shallower if your knees are sensitive, and swap to glute bridges if it still doesn't feel right. Tonestep asks about your knees when it builds your plan and serves gentler leg moves, with less deep bending, if you need them. Knee pain that lingers is worth showing a doctor or physical therapist.

If you're pregnant, your balance changes as your body does, so keep a hand on something steady and check with your doctor or midwife first.
