How to do a plank

Soft knees are fine. Keep breathing.

With coach Lena

Works
Core, shoulders
Beginners start with
a 20 to 30 second hold
Equipment
None

Step by step

  1. Elbows under shoulders Forearms or hands on the mat, feet back.
  2. One straight line Head to heels, hips level with your shoulders.
  3. Keep breathing Slow breaths in and out. Soft knees are fine.
  4. Belly tight Don't let your hips sag or pike up.

Common mistakes

  • Hips sagging. Your lower back takes over and starts to ache. Squeeze your glutes, tighten your belly and bring your hips level with your shoulders.
  • Hips piking up. A plank with your bottom in the air is resting, not holding. Lower until you make one line from head to heels.
  • Holding your breath. Slow breaths in and out the whole time. If you can't breathe, the hold is too hard; go shorter.
  • Sinking into your shoulders. Keep your elbows under your shoulders and gently push the floor away, so your upper back stays full rather than collapsing between your shoulder blades.
  • Chasing the clock. Stop when your line breaks. Three good 20-second holds do more than one sagging minute.

Muscles it works and why it matters

The plank works your whole core, the deep muscles that wrap around your middle, the ones along your sides and your back, with your shoulders and glutes helping hold you still. Its job is to keep your body from bending, which is what a strong middle does all day: when you carry shopping, lift a child or stand for a long time. It's also the base of every push-up, which is really a plank that moves.

How many and how often

Start with a 20-second hold with good form, two or three times, resting in between. Add a few seconds each session. A full minute is a good goal, and it's the finish line of Tonestep's 21-day core program. Two or three days a week is plenty. If 20 seconds is too much today, start with the bird dog; when a minute feels steady, the side plank and hollow hold come next.

Who it's good for, and when to go easier

Anyone can plank at some level: on your forearms or your hands, and with your knees down if you need them. A forearm plank keeps weight off your wrists, so try that first if your wrists are sensitive. If a shoulder hurts, stop for the day.

If you're pregnant or have had a baby in the last year, check with your doctor, midwife or a pelvic health physical therapist before you plank. If you notice your belly bulging into a ridge down the middle while you hold, stop, and ask one of them about it before you carry on.
