How to do a knee push-up

Hips in line with your shoulders. Lower slowly.

With coach Sarah

Works
Chest, triceps
Beginners start with
8 reps
Equipment
None

Step by step

  1. Knees on the mat Hands under your shoulders, feet lifted behind you.
  2. Make a straight line From your head to your knees, no bend at the hips.
  3. Lower slowly Chest toward the mat, hips stay in line.
  4. Press up strong Push the floor away until your arms are straight.

Common mistakes

  • Bending at the hips. Your bottom lifts and the push-up turns into a bow. Squeeze your glutes and keep one straight line from your head to your knees.
  • Knees too close to your hands. It shortens the lever until the push-up is barely there. Walk your hands forward until your body is long, and kneel just above your kneecaps.
  • Dropping down fast. Lower for a slow count of two or three. The way down is where much of the strength gets built.
  • Elbows flared straight out. Let them travel back at an angle rather than out to the sides, so your shoulders stay comfortable.
  • Stopping halfway. Bring your chest close to the mat and press back to straight arms. A few full reps beat many short ones.

Muscles it works and why it matters

Knee push-ups work your chest and the backs of your arms, with your shoulders and core holding the line. They are real push-ups: the same muscles, the same pattern, less of your bodyweight. For most women this is the rung that builds the strength for the floor, so it's worth doing well rather than getting past quickly.

How many and how often

Start with 8 reps, two or three sets, two or three days a week with a rest day in between. When all 8 are clean for two sessions in a row, start each set with one or two full push-ups and finish the rest on your knees. Over a few weeks the number on your toes grows. If 8 is too many today, step back to the incline push-up and build there.

Who it's good for, and when to go easier

It's the right rung once incline push-ups feel easy. Kneeling can be hard on knees: a folded towel or cushion under them helps, and if kneeling hurts, incline push-ups train the same muscles without it. Tonestep asks about your knees and wrists when it builds your plan and leaves out the moves that load them if you need a break.

Your hands carry real weight here, so keep them under your shoulders with your fingers spread. Wrist or shoulder pain that lingers is worth showing a doctor or physical therapist. If you're pregnant or have recently had a baby, ask your doctor or midwife before you start.
