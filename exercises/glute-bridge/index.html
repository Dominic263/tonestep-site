How to do a glute bridge

Push through your heels. Squeeze at the top.

With coach Sarah

Works
Glutes, hamstrings
Beginners start with
12 reps
Equipment
None

Step by step

  1. Lie on your back Knees bent, feet flat, hip-width apart, arms by your sides.
  2. Push through your heels Lift your hips until shoulders, hips and knees line up.
  3. Squeeze at the top Hold for a beat.
  4. Lower slowly Back down one vertebra at a time.

Common mistakes

  • Arching your lower back at the top. Lift until your shoulders, hips and knees make one line, then stop. Going higher bends your back, not your hips. Keep your ribs down and squeeze your glutes.
  • Feeling it mostly in your hamstrings. If the backs of your thighs cramp or do all the work, walk your heels a little closer to your hips.
  • Pushing through your toes. Push through your heels. Lifting your toes slightly off the floor is a good way to check.
  • Knees falling in or out. Keep your feet and knees hip-width apart, knees pointing the same way as your toes.
  • Dropping down. Hold for a beat at the top, then lower slowly, one vertebra at a time. The squeeze and the slow lowering are the exercise.

Muscles it works and why it matters

The glute bridge works your glutes, the biggest muscles in your body, with help from your hamstrings. Your glutes drive your hips forward, which is what powers standing up, climbing stairs and walking uphill. After a day of sitting they've been still for hours, and the bridge is a simple way to feel them working again. It's also the base of most at-home glute training.

How many and how often

Start with 12 reps, two or three sets, holding a beat at the top of each rep. Two or three days a week with a rest day in between is plenty to make progress. When 12 feel easy, hold the top for three seconds and take three seconds to lower. Then try the next rung, the donkey kick; further up the ladder, the single-leg glute bridge makes each side work on its own.

Who it's good for, and when to go easier

It's a great first move: no weight on your wrists, little demand on your knees, and you can't fall over. That makes it a good choice if squats or lunges don't feel right yet. If your lower back aches, lift a little lower and focus on the squeeze; back pain that sticks around is worth showing a doctor or physical therapist.

The bridge is done lying on your back. Many pregnancy guidelines suggest avoiding long stretches flat on your back as pregnancy goes on, so if you're pregnant, ask your doctor or midwife whether it's right for you and what to do instead.
