How to do a squat

Sit back like there's a chair behind you.

With coach Jess

Works
Thighs, glutes
Beginners start with
12 reps
Equipment
None

Step by step

  1. Stand tall Feet hip-width apart, toes forward, hands at your chest.
  2. Sit back and down Hips back, like reaching for a chair.
  3. Thighs parallel, chest up Heels stay down and your back stays long.
  4. Knees follow your toes Don't let them cave inward.
  5. Drive up through your heels Push the floor away and stand tall.

Common mistakes

  • Knees caving in. Your knees should follow your toes. Think about gently pushing them out as you sit back, especially on the way up.
  • Heels lifting. Keep your weight through your heels and the middle of your feet; you should be able to wiggle your toes at the bottom. If your heels still lift, don't sink as deep for now. Depth comes with practice.
  • Chest folding toward your knees. Keep your hands at your chest, your eyes forward and your back long, so your hips and chest move together.
  • Squatting partway, or dropping fast. Aim for thighs about parallel to the floor, lowering for a count of two. If that's not there yet, squat to a chair: sit back until you touch it, then stand.
  • Bouncing at the bottom. Pause for a moment, then drive up through your heels to standing. Control beats speed.

Muscles it works and why it matters

The squat works your thighs, front and back, and your glutes, with your calves and core helping you balance. It's the movement behind sitting down and standing up, climbing stairs and picking things up off the floor, so getting stronger at it shows up all day, not just in workouts. Done well, it's one of the most useful moves you can learn.

How many and how often

Start with 12 reps, two or three sets, two or three days a week with a rest day in between. Tonestep can count your squats with your iPhone's camera, so you can keep your eyes up instead of counting. When 12 feel easy, slow the way down to three seconds or pause at the bottom before you add more reps. The next rung up is the reverse lunge, which works one leg at a time.

Who it's good for, and when to go easier

Almost everyone can squat to some depth, and it's the first legs move in Tonestep for that reason. If your knees are sensitive, squat to a chair or a higher seat and keep the range comfortable; some muscle burn is normal, sharp or pinching pain in a joint is not. If that kind of pain keeps happening, stop and see a doctor or physical therapist.

Holding a counter or the back of a sturdy chair is fine if your balance isn't sure yet. If you're pregnant or recently gave birth, check with your doctor or midwife before starting a new routine.
