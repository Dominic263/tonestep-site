Stronger legs in 21 days

Three weeks from your base to strong with Jess: squats and lunges on steady knees, stairs that feel easier, and a wall sit that holds.

By the Tonestep team · Updated October 23, 2026

By day 21

  • Squats and lunges on steady knees
  • Stairs and hills that feel easier
  • A wall sit that holds

Squats and lunges at your rung first, slow and steady, then longer wall sits and slower lunges, then your longest holds and most reps yet. Stairs, hills and getting up off the floor feel easier on the way.

Who this is for

This is for women who want stronger legs for everyday life: if stairs leave your thighs burning, if getting up from a low chair takes a push from your hands, or if squats and lunges feel wobbly. You don't need any equipment, and nothing in it jumps, so it suits apartments and early mornings. You start at the rung that's right for you; a squat to a chair counts. Jess leads it, the coach who teaches legs in Tonestep: “Strong legs carry you everywhere. Every stair will thank you.”

How the three weeks build

Three weeks, from your base to strong. The plan starts with squats and reverse lunges at your rung, done slowly so you learn what a good rep feels like. Then it asks you to hold on a little longer: wall sits a few seconds more each time, and lunges with a slower way down. The last week brings your longest holds and most reps yet. Along the way, split squats work one leg at a time, so your stronger side can't quietly take over, and calf raises strengthen the lower legs that carry you up every stair.

Workouts take about 20 minutes on the days you choose to train. Every move sits on a ladder, so on a hard day Make it easier serves a gentler version and the workout carries on. If your knees need a break, tell Tonestep to go easy on them and it swaps in moves that ask less of them.

What to expect along the way

The first week. Your thighs and glutes will probably let you know about it a day or two after your first workouts, especially going down stairs. Mild soreness is normal when muscles do something new, and it fades as they get used to it. A little shaking in a wall sit is normal too.

The second week. Holds get longer and lunges get steadier. You may notice it outside workouts first: on the stairs, or standing up from the floor without reaching for something.

The third week. Your strongest legs of the three weeks, and a wall sit that holds where it used to give out. Where you finish depends on where you started; a deeper squat or a longer hold than in week one is real progress, and the strength you built is yours to keep building.

Tips that make it stick

  • Knees follow your toes. In squats and lunges, keep your knees pointing the same way as your feet, and gently push them out on the way up.
  • Go only as deep as feels good. Depth comes with practice. A chair behind you for squats, or a hand on the wall in lunges, is fine while you learn.
  • Slow down on the way down. Lowering for a count of two or three makes every rep count for more.
  • Let your phone count. Prop it up and the camera counts your squats, so your eyes can stay forward.
  • Know burn from pain. Muscle burn is normal; sharp or pinching pain in a knee or hip is not. If that keeps happening, stop and see a doctor or physical therapist. If you're pregnant or recently had a baby, check with your doctor or midwife before you start.

Frequently asked questions

Can you build stronger legs without weights?

Yes, especially as a beginner. Squats, lunges and wall sits load your legs well, and slower reps and longer holds make them harder without adding anything.

How long should a beginner hold a wall sit?

Start with 20 to 30 seconds, sliding down only as far as feels good; you don't need to reach a right angle. Add a few seconds each session.

What if squats or lunges bother my knees?

Squat to a chair, make lunges shallower, and keep your knees following your toes. Tonestep can go easy on your knees and swap in moves that ask less of them. Knee pain that lingers is worth showing a doctor or physical therapist.
