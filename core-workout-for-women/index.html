A stronger core in 21 days

Three weeks from steady to strong with Lena: stand taller, feel steadier in everything you do, and hold a plank for a full minute.

By the Tonestep team · Updated October 23, 2026

By day 21

  • Hold a plank for a full minute
  • Stand and sit taller
  • A stronger middle for every other move

Control first with bird dogs and reverse crunches, then longer planks and hollow holds, then your strongest holds yet. A stronger middle makes every other move easier.

Who this is for

This is for women who want a stronger, steadier middle: if planks shake after ten seconds, if you sit at a desk all day, or if moves like push-ups and lunges feel wobbly. You don't need a solid plank to start; the first week begins with slow, controlled moves on the floor. Lena leads it, the coach who teaches core in Tonestep: “A strong middle makes every other move easier. We'll get there slowly, and properly.”

How the three weeks build

Three weeks, from steady to strong. The plan starts with control: slow moves like the bird dog that teach your middle to stay still while your arms and legs move. Then the holds get longer, a few seconds at a time, with planks and hollow holds. The last week brings your strongest holds yet, building toward a plank of a full minute.

Workouts take about 20 minutes on the days you choose to train. Every move sits on a ladder, so on a hard day an easier one (a bird dog in place of a plank, say) is always one tap away with Make it easier.

What to expect along the way

The first week. Slow moves feel surprisingly hard. Shaking during a hold is normal: it's your muscles working, not a sign you're doing it wrong. Some soreness across your belly a day or two later is common too.

The second week. Holds get longer and your form gets steadier. You may notice you're standing and sitting a little taller without thinking about it.

The third week. Your longest planks yet. A full minute is the goal; if you finish at 40 or 45 seconds with your hips level, that's real progress from where you started, and the minute is close. A stronger core makes your middle firmer and steadier, but it doesn't burn fat from your stomach. What changes is strength, posture and how solid you feel in everything else you do.

Tips that make it stick

  • Breathe through every hold. If you can't breathe, the hold is too hard; go shorter.
  • Stop when the line breaks. A sagging plank hands the work to your lower back. End the hold, rest, then try again.
  • Add seconds, not minutes. A few more seconds each session adds up quickly over three weeks.
  • Use it everywhere. A steadier middle helps your push-ups and lunges; the first push-up plan uses planks for exactly that reason.
  • After a baby, ask first. If you've had a baby in the last year, a pelvic health physical therapist can tell you when planks and other core work are right for you.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a beginner hold a plank?

Start with 20 to 30 seconds with good form, hips level. Add a few seconds each session.

Will a core workout flatten my stomach?

Core training makes your middle stronger and steadier; it doesn't burn fat from one spot. Strength, posture and how you stand are what change.
