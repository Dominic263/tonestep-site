Firmer, stronger glutes in 4 weeks

A 4-week glute transformation with Maya: lift, shape and strengthen, at home, with no equipment and nothing that jumps.

By the Tonestep team · Updated October 23, 2026

By day 28

  • Firmer, stronger glutes
  • Squats and stairs that feel easy
  • Hips that feel open after a day of sitting

Four weeks that get harder as you get stronger: waking your glutes up, holding longer, one leg at a time, then everything together. Squats and stairs start to feel easy, and your hips feel open after a day of sitting.

Who this is for

This is for women who want stronger, firmer glutes without a gym or any equipment, starting from wherever they are. If you sit for most of the day, if stairs leave your legs complaining, or if you need workouts quiet enough for an apartment, it fits: nothing in it jumps. It's led by Maya, who teaches glutes in Tonestep. In her words: “Strong is a feeling, not a size. We'll build it one bridge at a time.”

How the four weeks build

The plan mixes floor work, like bridges and kicks, with standing work, like squats and lunges, and gets harder each week. It starts by teaching you to feel your glutes doing the work, which is harder than it sounds after years of sitting. Then it makes each rep count for more, with slower reps and holds rather than piles of extra reps. Then it works one leg at a time, so each side does its share. The last week puts everything together at your strongest.

Every move sits on a ladder, so if something is too much on a given day, Make it easier serves a gentler version and the workout carries on.

What to expect along the way

The first week. You'll probably feel your glutes more than you expect, during the moves and a day or two after. That's a good sign you're finding the right muscles. If you feel bridges mostly in your hamstrings or lower back, a small change to where your feet are usually fixes it; the glute bridge guide shows how.

The middle weeks. Holds get longer, and one-leg work shows you which side is stronger. It's normal for one side to lag at first. Squats and stairs often start to feel easier around here.

The end. Glutes that are stronger and that you can feel working: in how you climb stairs, how you stand up from a chair, and how your hips feel after a day of sitting. How your shape changes, and how quickly, depends on more than four weeks of training, including food, sleep and your own build, so let strength be the measure. A weekly progress photo, kept only on your phone, can show what the mirror misses day to day.

Tips that make it stick

  • Squeeze at the top. Pause for a beat at the top of every bridge and kick. That's where much of the work happens.
  • Slow down instead of speeding up. A slow lowering does more for your glutes than extra fast reps.
  • Feel it in the right place. If your lower back is doing the work, make the movement smaller and squeeze harder.
  • Keep your rest days. Two or three sessions a week, with a day off between, is plenty to make progress.
  • Keep moving on the days off. Walking or other gentle movement you enjoy is great on the days in between.

Frequently asked questions

Can you build glutes without weights?

Yes, especially as a beginner. Bridges, kicks and single-leg moves load the glutes well, and slower reps make them harder.

How often should I train glutes?

Two or three times a week, with a rest day between, is plenty to see progress.
