28-day transformation

Your first full push-up in 28 days

Four weeks from wall push-ups to the floor, and stronger, more toned arms, shoulders and core on the way.

By the Tonestep team · Updated October 23, 2026

By day 28

  • Your first full push-up
  • Toned arms and shoulders
  • A strong plank to build on

Most women who can't do a push-up yet aren't far off: they just started on the floor, which is the hardest rung. This plan starts against a wall and climbs, one rung at a time. In the last week, every workout ends with a try at a full push-up.

Who this is for

This plan is for women who can't do a full push-up yet, or can do one on a good day and want to own it. You don't need to do anything on the floor to start: if a wall push-up is where you are today, that's where the plan begins. It also suits anyone coming back after a long break who wants one clear goal and short workouts, about 20 minutes on the days you choose to train. Sarah leads it, the coach who takes you from the wall up.

How the four weeks build

The plan climbs the push-up ladder: the wall, then an incline on a counter or couch, then your knees, then the floor. Each week asks a little more than the last. First you find the rung that's right for you and get comfortable there, then you do more on that rung, then you step one rung closer to the floor. Alongside the push-ups you strengthen what holds a push-up together, your shoulders and your core, because a push-up is a plank that moves. In the last week, every workout ends with a try at a full push-up.

Your rung is yours. If a day feels too hard, tap Make it easier; when the reps feel easy, tap Make it harder. The plan remembers where you are.

What to expect along the way

The first week. You'll probably feel your chest, arms and shoulders a day or two after your first workouts. Mild soreness is normal when muscles do something new, and it fades as they get used to it. The main job this week is learning what a good rep feels like.

The middle weeks. Reps that felt hard start to feel ordinary. You may notice it outside workouts first: getting up off the floor, carrying bags, holding a plank a little longer. This is usually when you move up a rung.

The last week. The try at a full push-up. Some women get one on the first attempt, some later in the week, and some finish on their knees, a rung or two higher than where they started. Where you finish depends on where you began. If the floor isn't there on day 28, keep going: you're closer than you were, and the strength you built is still yours.

Tips that make it stick

  • Pick your days and protect them. Two or three workouts a week on days you can actually keep beat a perfect plan you skip.
  • Form first. One straight line from head to heels, chest close to the floor, all the way back up. If the line breaks, step down a rung for that set.
  • Slow down on the way down. Lowering for a count of three is one of the best ways to build strength for the floor.
  • Let rest days work. Strength builds between sessions, and a rest day in your plan never breaks your streak.
  • Let your phone count. Prop it up and the camera counts your push-ups, so your eyes can stay on the floor just ahead of your hands.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't I do a push-up yet?

A floor push-up asks you to press most of your bodyweight. Starting on an easier angle (a wall, then a counter or couch) builds the same strength first.

Are knee push-ups real push-ups?

Yes. They train the same muscles in the same pattern with less load, and they're a step on the way to the floor.

Will this tone my arms?

Push-ups work your chest, shoulders and the backs of your arms. Four weeks of steady practice makes them stronger and firmer; how they look also depends on things like sleep and food.
